### 1. Theoretical Foundation: The Method of Characteristics
In hyperbolic systems like the SWE, information travels along characteristic lines at specific velocities:
*   **$\lambda_1 = u + c$** (Information moving downstream)
*   **$\lambda_2 = u - c$** (Information moving upstream)
*   *Where $u$ is advective velocity and $c = \sqrt{gh}$ is celerity.*

**The Golden Rule of Well-Posedness:** For a numerical solution to be stable and unique, you must provide exactly one boundary condition (instruction) for every characteristic entering the domain. 
*   In **Subcritical Flow** ($u < c$), one characteristic enters from the left and one from the right.
*   **Requirement:** You **must** provide exactly one BC at the inlet and one BC at the outlet.

---

### 2. Evaluated Configurations

#### A. The "Pressure-Driven" Sloping Channel (Initial Phase)
*   **Inlet ($x=0$):** Fixed Elevation ($\zeta = 0$).
*   **Outlet ($x=L$):** Fixed Elevation ($\zeta = -1.0$).
*   **Physics:** Driven by a fixed "Head" (1-meter drop). Gravity pushes the water until bed friction balances the slope.
*   **Result:** Reaches "Normal Depth." Velocity is an *output* of the system, not a control.
*   **Drawback:** Hard to simulate specific tidal currents ($U_{in}$) because the head is the master.

#### B. The "Ill-Posed" Inlet-Only Forcing (The Failure Case)
*   **Inlet ($x=0$):** Fixed Discharge ($q$) **and** Fixed Elevation ($\zeta$).
*   **Outlet ($x=L$):** Floating/Zero-Gradient (Neumann).
*   **Result:** **Numerical Evacuation (Blow-up).**
*   **Physics:** Without a downstream "anchor" (fixed $\zeta$), the upstream-moving characteristic ($u-c$) enters from the "Sea" with no instructions. Gravity pulls water out, and the outlet simply allows it to drain. 
*   **Numerical Signature:** Depth ($h \to 0$), Velocity ($u \to \infty$), Froude Number ($Fr \to 3000+$).

#### C. The "Flux-Forced Flume" (Tidal Standard - Final Phase)
*   **Inlet ($x=0$):** Fixed Discharge ($q = U_{target} \cdot H_{swl}$). Elevation ($\zeta$) floats (Zero-Gradient).
*   **Outlet ($x=L$):** Fixed Elevation ($\zeta = 0.0$ at Geoid). Discharge ($q$) floats (Zero-Gradient).
*   **Physics:** This represents a tidal strait. You "pump" a specific flux into the channel. The inlet elevation naturally rises (Backwater Effect) to create the head needed to push that flux over the friction and bathymetry.
*   **Result:** Extremely stable. Velocity at the inlet ($u_0$) adjusts based on the "piling up" of water.

---

### 3. Key Conceptual Distinctions
*   **The "Driver":** Flow is driven by the gradient of the free surface ($\partial \zeta / \partial x$), not necessarily the bed slope. In a flat channel ($S_0=0$), the water level rises at the inlet to create the downhill slope needed to move the water.
*   **Dirichlet vs. Neumann:** 
    *   *Dirichlet:* Specifying the value (e.g., $\zeta = 0$). Acts as a physical "anchor."
    *   *Neumann:* Specifying the gradient (e.g., $q_i = q_{i+1}$). Acts as a "transparent window" allowing flow to pass.
*   **Static Pond State:** By setting $U_{in} = 0$ in the Flux-Forced model, the water reaches a perfectly horizontal equilibrium with the outlet sea level. This is a vital "Zero-State" for AI training.

### 4. Implementation Logic (RK4 Snippet)
```python
# Inlet: The "Pump" (Tidal Flux)
self.q[0] = self.q_target_in         # Dirichlet (Specified Flux)
self.zeta[0] = self.zeta[1]          # Neumann (Floating Elevation)

# Outlet: The "Sea" (Downstream Datum)
self.zeta[-1] = 0.0                  # Dirichlet (Fixed Sea Level)
self.q[-1] = self.q[-2]              # Neumann (Transmissive Exit)
```
